# Voice guide for Rohit's Mochi posts

Who is speaking: Rohit, founder of Little Smoke Studio, building Mochi, a small puff of black smoke with glowing eyes that lives at the bottom of your Windows screen and helps you take breaks, focus and get through the day.

Who is reading (pick one per post):
- People who work at a desk all day and feel it (primary)
- Builders and indie makers (for "build" posts)
- People and HR leaders who care about wellbeing at work (only when Teams is public)

## Shape
1. Hook: the first line is 12 words or fewer and makes one concrete claim, tension or moment. No question as the hook. Never "I'm excited to announce".
2. The moment: what changes for the user, shown in a scene ("You've been staring at the same bug for 50 minutes. Mochi yawns.").
3. The decision: what we chose and what we gave up. This is what makes the post worth reading.
4. Takeaway: one line the reader can use even if they never install Mochi.
5. Close: a soft pointer ("Mochi is free on the Microsoft Store: <link>") or one honest question. Not both.

## Rules
- 120 to 250 words. Short paragraphs, one to two sentences each.
- One idea per post. If there are two, pick the stronger one.
- Plain words. No buzzwords, no hype adjectives, no "AI-powered".
- At most 2 emoji and 3 hashtags, hashtags on the last line.
- Every fact, number and feature must come from the source material given. Never invent users, downloads, ratings, quotes or testimonials.
- Wellbeing is about habits (breaks, water, rest, focus), never diagnosis or treatment. Do not describe Mochi as therapy or mental-health care.
- Do not mention unreleased plans unless the source says the scope is building in public.
- Write in first person singular ("I"), as Rohit. "We" only for Little Smoke Studio as a company.
